Privacy policy for Abhyas

Last updated: 25 October 2026

Abhyas does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, and no server to send anything to.

This is not a promise that rests on good intentions. The app declares no internet permission at all, and explicitly removes the one that its libraries would otherwise have added. An app without that permission is prevented by Android itself from making a network connection. You can verify this yourself: Android lists an app’s permissions on the Play Store listing and in Settings, and Abhyas asks for exactly two.

What the app stores, and where

Everything Abhyas keeps is stored on your device, in the app’s own private storage, which no other app can read.

  • Your decks and cards — the questions, answers, and the sentence each card was made from.
  • Photos for image-occlusion cards — when you make a card that hides parts of a picture, that picture is kept privately inside the app’s own storage. It is deleted when the card, or the deck it belongs to, is deleted.
  • Your review history — when you answered a card and how you graded it. This is what draws your streak and your retention figures.
  • Your settings — whether you asked for a daily reminder, and at what time.
  • A crash log — if the app crashes, a record of the error is written to the device so the problem can be diagnosed. It stays on your device and is never sent automatically. Under “Send diagnostics” in Settings you can view it, delete it, or choose to email it to support yourself, through your own mail app — in which case you see exactly what is sent and to whom before anything leaves the phone.

Uninstalling the app deletes all of it. There is no copy anywhere else.

The two permissions, and why

  • Camera — to photograph a page of your notes. The photograph is read on your device by an offline text recogniser that ships inside the app, and the file is deleted as soon as the text has been extracted. Abhyas keeps the text, not the photograph. Nothing is uploaded, and no photograph is written to your gallery. (The one exception is a photo you choose to turn into an image-occlusion card, which is kept in the app’s private storage as described above.)
  • Notifications — only if you switch on the daily reminder, which is off until you turn it on. The reminder is generated on your device; nothing is received from anywhere.

You may also pick an existing image from your gallery instead of taking a photograph. Abhyas reads the image you choose and nothing else, and never deletes or modifies a file you already had.

Text recognition and speech are offline

The text recognition models are bundled inside the app rather than downloaded or called over the network. Photographing a page works with the phone in aeroplane mode, and that is the test of whether an offline claim is real.

When Abhyas reads a card aloud, it uses the text-to-speech engine already installed on your device, working offline. Abhyas itself sends nothing anywhere to produce speech.

Backups you make yourself

Settings offers an export that writes your whole collection to a single file, at a location you choose. That file is yours: Abhyas does not read it again unless you ask it to restore from it, and does not send it anywhere. If you place it in a folder that a cloud service syncs, that service’s own privacy policy governs the copy it takes.

Android’s own automatic backup is switched off for this app, so your collection is not silently copied to your Google account.

Children

Abhyas does not collect data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If a future version collects anything, this policy will be updated before that version is published, and the change will be described here rather than quietly made.
